Gas vs EV cost by state

At the US average residential electricity price of 18.17 cents per kWh (September 2026), a 2026 Tesla Model Y Long Range RWD (25 kWh per 100 miles) costs 4.5 cents a mile to charge at home. A 2026 Honda CR-V 1.5 L turbo, FWD (30 mpg) at the $3.642 national gasoline average costs 12.1 cents a mile. The electricity to drive the EV as far as the CR-V goes on one gallon, its eGallon, is $1.36. [EIA · September 2026 electricity, November 23, 2026 gasoline] [FuelEconomy.gov · 25 Nov 2026]

On these two ratings, home charging is cheaper per mile in 51 of 51 jurisdictions. The gap is widest in Washington (3.8 against 15.3 cents a mile, gasoline on EIA's own series for the state) and narrowest in Hawaii (12.1 against 14.2 cents a mile, gasoline on the EIA West Coast less California average).

Method

EV cost per mile = residential electricity price x the EV's EPA combined kWh per 100 miles / 100. Gas cost per mile = gasoline price / the gas car's EPA combined MPG. eGallon = electricity price x kWh per mile x the gas car's MPG, the cost of driving the EV the distance the gas car covers on one gallon, so it compares directly with the price of a gallon. The pair is two 2026 compact SUVs: the Honda CR-V 1.5 L turbo, FWD and the Tesla Model Y Long Range RWD (ratings read on fueleconomy.gov, November 25, 2026).

Residential prices from EIA Electric Power Monthly Table 5.6.A are monthly averages for the whole state and include no time-of-use or EV tariff. Public charging is priced by each network, not by these residential averages, so the figures here are for charging at home.
