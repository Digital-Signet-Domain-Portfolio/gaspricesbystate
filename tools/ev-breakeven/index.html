WASHINGTON, August 31 -

Gas vs EV cost by state

An EV equivalent gallon (eGallon) is what it would cost in electricity to drive the same distance a 28-mpg gasoline car would on one gallon. At the US average residential electric rate of 16.5 c/kWh that's about $5.56 per eGallon, well under today's $4.07 gallon. Hawaii is the only state where electricity makes the math closer. Method

Residential rates from EIA EPM Table 5.6.A. eGallon = elec rate (cents/kWh) x 33.7 kWh. The 33.7 kWh conversion is the EPA MPGe convention. Real EV efficiency varies (3-4 miles/kWh typical), so eGallon understates real EV savings for most cars.

Where EVs barely win

Hawaii (41 c/kWh) is the only state where the eGallon is close to the gasoline price. Connecticut, Massachusetts, Rhode Island, California, and Alaska have the highest residential rates but their gasoline prices are also high, so the EV margin holds.
