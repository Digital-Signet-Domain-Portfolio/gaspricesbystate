LOS ANGELES, August 31 -

Gas prices California vs Texas

In the EIA survey week of August 31, 2026, California regular averaged $5.52 per gallon and Texas $3.58 per gallon. Both states carry their own weekly federal series, so the $1.94-per-gallon gap is measured, not inferred. [EIA · 31 Aug 2026]

What the federal survey settles here. Both states have their own EIA weekly retail series (EMM_EPMR_PTE_SCA_DPG, EMM_EPMR_PTE_STX_DPG), so the difference below is a measured one from the week of August 31, 2026.

Why California costs $1.94 more

  • CARB summer blend: ~20-30 cents/gal premium over conventional gasoline.
  • State excise 63.4 c/gal vs Texas 20 c/gal: ~43 cents/gal difference.
  • Cap-and-trade plus LCFS: ~25-40 cents/gal.
  • Sales tax (CA applies general sales tax to fuel): ~25-35 cents/gal at this price.
  • Refining margin: PADD 5 cracks structurally wider than PADD 3.
  • No pipeline from Gulf to California: every gallon comes from in-state refineries or tanker imports.

Per-tank difference

For a 26-gallon pickup, the per-fill difference is $50.44. For a 15-gallon sedan, $29.10. Annual cost (15,000 miles, 25 mpg): $1,164 more in California.

The gap this week

Series EMM_EPMR_PTE_SCA_DPG and EMM_EPMR_PTE_STX_DPG are the two figures above; the $1.94/gal difference is what the federal survey recorded in the week of August 31, 2026. Climate fees and CARB-blend costs are the structural reason the gap persists.
