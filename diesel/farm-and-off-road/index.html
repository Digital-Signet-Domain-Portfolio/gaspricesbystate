What dyed diesel is

Dyed diesel is diesel fuel marked at the terminal to show federal excise has not been paid on it. The dye is Solvent Red 164, which is why it is called red diesel (26 CFR 48.4082-1). The dye marks tax status; diesel fuel sold in the US is held to EPA's 15 ppm sulfur standard whether or not it is dyed, with narrow exceptions (40 CFR 1090.300 and 1090.305). [EPA · 23 Nov 2026]

Who can legally use it

IRS Publication 510 lists the nontaxable uses that dyed diesel is meant for. The main ones:

  • Use on a farm for farming purposes
  • Off-highway business use, such as construction equipment that is not registered for the road
  • Use as heating oil
  • Exclusive use by a state or local government

Putting it in a vehicle driven on a public highway is a taxable use, whoever the buyer is. [IRS · 23 Nov 2026]

Penalty for misuse

Federal: the greater of $1,000 or $10 per gallon of dyed fuel involved, for each violation. The $1,000 floor is multiplied up for anyone already penalised before (26 U.S.C. 6715(b)). States set penalties of their own on top. [IRS · 23 Nov 2026]

Getting an actual price

Off-road diesel is sold by local fuel distributors, usually delivered in bulk, and their prices are not published in any federal series. Ask two or three distributors for a delivered price per gallon at your volume, and compare it with the reference above for your EIA district.

UK red diesel price by month

In the UK, red diesel is the trade name for marked gas oil, diesel that carries a rebated rate of duty and may be used only in excepted vehicles and machines, such as agricultural vehicles used for farming (HMRC Excise Notice 75). [HMRC · read 25 Nov 2026] No official series prices it daily.
